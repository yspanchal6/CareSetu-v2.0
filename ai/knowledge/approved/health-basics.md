# Health Basics & Emergency Help (approved seed content)

## When to call 108 immediately
Call the free Emergency Response Support System (108) right away if you or someone near you shows:
chest pain that does not go away, trouble breathing, sudden confusion, fainting, heavy bleeding,
or signs of a stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech). Stay on the line, speak
slowly, and give your exact location.

## First aid for a small burn
Cool the burn under running water for at least 10 minutes. Do not apply ice, butter, or paste.
Cover loosely with a clean cloth and avoid popping any blisters. See a doctor if the burn is
larger than your palm, on the face, or painful for more than a day.

## Handling a nosebleed
Sit upright, lean slightly forward, and pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 minutes.
Do not tilt your head back. If bleeding continues past 20 minutes or is heavy, seek care.

## Common cold vs allergy
A cold usually comes with mild fever, sore throat, and a runny nose lasting under two weeks.
Allergies often cause sneezing and itchy eyes that last longer without fever. Rest, fluids,
and simple paracetamol help; see a doctor if symptoms worsen.

## When a headache needs attention
See a doctor promptly if a headache comes on suddenly and very severely, follows a head injury,
or is accompanied by neck stiffness, fever, confusion, or weakness on one side.

## Medication safety
Never double a dose to make up for a missed one. Keep medicines in their original packs and
tell your doctor about everything you take, including supplements. If in doubt, ask a
pharmacist or 108 guidance.

## Heat stroke warning signs
High body temperature, hot dry skin, confusion, and rapid breathing in hot weather can signal
heat stroke — an emergency. Move the person to shade, cool them with water, and call 108.
